    How the trip safety check works

    Formula & FAQ +

    It first checks the hours: driving time is miles divided by your realistic speed, plus a 30-minute break for every 8 hours of driving. If that does not fit in your remaining 11-hour and 14-hour limits, the trip is not legal as planned.

    Then it adds up risk factors that make crashes more likely: short sleep, driving between 2 and 6 a.m. when the body is least alert, long runs, bad weather, mountain grades, heavy traffic, heavy or hazmat loads, unfamiliar roads and tight appointments. More factors means a higher risk level and more specific advice.

    driving time = miles ÷ average speed
    30-minute breaks = one each time driving since the last break would pass 8 hours
    risk points: sleep under 5 h = 3, under 7 h = 1, 2–6 a.m. driving = 2 (3 if 2 hours or more), snow/ice = 2, each other factor = 1
    0–2 low · 3–4 medium · 5+ high · out of hours = not legal

    Does this replace my ELD?

    No. Your ELD is the legal record. This is a quick plan before you accept a load.

    Why does 2 to 6 a.m. matter?

    That is when the body's alertness is naturally lowest, even for drivers used to night runs, so fatigue-related crashes are more likely.
