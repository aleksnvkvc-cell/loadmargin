UCR, the $46 filing that stops you at the scale

It is the cheapest compliance item there is and one of the most commonly forgotten. Here is who owes it, the 2026 brackets, the deadline, and why an unpaid UCR turns into a roadside problem.

Unified Carrier Registration is an annual fee that interstate carriers, brokers and freight forwarders pay into a shared federal-state system. It is small — $46 for a one-truck operation — but it is enforced at the roadside, and an unpaid UCR is a violation that can put you out of service.

What UCR is

UCR replaced an older system and funds state safety programmes. You register once a year, in your base state, and that covers you for interstate operation everywhere. It is not a permit for a specific trip and it is not the same as IFTA, IRP, HVUT or your operating authority — it is its own separate annual line item.

Four different annual obligations get confused constantly: UCR, IRP plates, IFTA and HVUT. They are separate filings with separate deadlines. Missing one is not covered by paying another.

Who has to register

You owe UCR if you operate in interstate commerce as any of:

  • A for-hire or private motor carrier of property or passengers
  • A freight forwarder or leasing company
  • A broker — even with no trucks, brokers pay the smallest bracket

Purely intrastate operations — never crossing a state line and not carrying interstate freight — are outside UCR. Almost every carrier with its own authority is inside it.

The 2026 fee brackets

The fee steps up with the size of your fleet:

Power units2026 fee
0–2$46
3–5$138
6–20$276
21–100$963
101–1,000$4,592
1,001 and up$44,836

A broker with no trucks and an owner-operator with one truck both sit in the bottom bracket at $46. The brackets are wide, so a growing small fleet stays at $276 all the way from six trucks to twenty.

Counting your power units

The bracket is set by the number of power units — trucks — not trailers. Two useful points:

  • You generally count the vehicles listed on your most recent MCS-150, so keep that biennial update accurate — an inflated truck count pushes you into a higher bracket.
  • Trailers do not count. A one-truck operation with three trailers is still bottom-bracket.

The deadline and enforcement

Registration opens 1 October and the fee is due by 31 December for the coming year. From 1 January, enforcement is active:

Unpaid UCR is a roadside matter, not a letter

States check UCR at inspections and scales. An unregistered carrier can be fined and put out of service until it registers. For a $46 filing that is a wildly bad trade, and it is entirely avoidable by putting the renewal in the calendar for October alongside your other year-end filings.

How to file without overpaying

  1. File on the official national UCR site or through your base state directly.
  2. Be wary of look-alike sites that add a service fee on top of the government fee. The registration itself is inexpensive; you are paying $46, not $150.
  3. Confirm your power-unit count before you pay, so you land in the right bracket.
  4. Save the confirmation and keep a copy in the truck.

Base state, and paying only once

You register in your base state and that one registration covers interstate operation nationwide. If your base state does not participate in UCR, you register through the nearest participating state. You do not pay each state you drive through — anyone telling you otherwise is confusing UCR with something else.

UCR (Dec 31), the Clearinghouse query (Jan 5) and your IFTA quarter all cluster around the turn of the year. Do them together and none of them slips.
