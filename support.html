If you have a spreadsheet

Keep it, and check it against the free calculator once. Spreadsheets do arithmetic perfectly well — they fail because they live on one laptop, they go stale silently when a cost changes, and nobody opens them mid-call. If a spreadsheet is genuinely what fits your operation, buy a good one rather than maintaining a bad one.

If you pay for rate data

Keep that too. Market rate data and your own cost model answer different questions — one tells you what a lane is paying, the other tells you whether you can afford to haul it. A carrier who knows both negotiates from a much stronger position than one who knows either.

If you run a TMS

A TMS runs your operation; this scores a decision. There is no live integration today and we will not imply otherwise — you enter loads by hand or import a backup file. Bulk import from a TMS export is on the roadmap and is not in the product.

What LoadMargin is not. It is not a TMS, an ELD, an accounting package, a load board, a rate index, or a compliance certification. It does not file your IFTA return, pay your drivers, or track your trucks. It answers one question — what does this load actually net — and it tries to answer it better than anything else you have open at that moment.
Getting started

Ten minutes, and most of it is finding one number

Find your monthly fixed cost

Truck and trailer payments, insurance, permits, parking, accounting, phone, and whatever you pay yourself that does not depend on miles. This is the part that takes the time, and it is the part everything else rests on.

Put it in the cost-per-mile builder

On the home page. Divide by the miles you actually run in a month — including empty ones. The result is usually 10 to 25 cents higher than people expect, and that gap is the entire reason to do this.

Click Apply to calculator

Your cost base now drives every calculation on the site and every module in the console.

Score a load you already ran

Not a hypothetical one. Take a real load from last week where you know what you were paid and roughly what it cost you, and compare. If the answer surprises you, that surprise is the whole point — and if it does not match your books, we want to hear about it.

Then open the console

The eight-module console is free and unlimited. Log a few weeks of loads and the lane and customer reports start telling you things a single load never could.

Your data

Where it lives, and how to keep it

Nothing is uploaded

Every calculation runs in your browser. Open your developer tools, watch the Network tab, and use the calculator — there is no outbound request carrying your figures. The console stores your fleet in your browser's local storage.

This has one real cost, and we would rather state it than bury it: we cannot recover your data, because we never had it.

So take backups

Clearing your browsing data, using private browsing, or switching browser will lose the fleet. Keep the JSON somewhere you would keep an invoice.

Team use. Each person can use the console free on their own device, but the data does not sync between them — it lives in each browser separately. The working method for now is that one person maintains the cost model and shares the exported JSON. Shared accounts do not exist yet and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

Troubleshooting

If something is not working

The numbers do not match my accountant

Most often this is the fixed cost per mile. Check two things: that your monthly overhead includes everything that does not depend on miles, and that you divided by total monthly miles including empty ones rather than by loaded miles. Dividing by loaded miles alone understates fixed cost per mile by whatever your deadhead percentage is.

My fleet data disappeared

The console stores everything in your browser's local storage, so clearing browsing data, using private browsing, or switching browser or device loses it. We cannot restore it — we never had a copy. Going forward, use Fleet & Data → Export backup and keep the JSON file.

A number shows as — or NaN

Something is empty or zero where it is used as a divider — usually MPG or monthly miles. Every calculator guards against dividing by zero rather than crashing, so it shows a dash instead of a wrong figure. Fill the field in and it recalculates immediately.

The page looks broken on my phone

Try a hard refresh first — an old cached stylesheet is the usual cause after an update. If it persists, tell us the phone, the browser and roughly the year of the device, and it gets tested against that.

A workbook shows #VALUE! or #REF!

That should not happen — every file ships with zero formula errors. If it does, the file is almost always a partial download or was opened in something that does not support the formula. Re-download first, then email us the sheet name and cell reference and it gets fixed and re-issued to everyone.

The workbook opens but the colours look wrong

Google Sheets renders a handful of conditional-formatting rules differently from Excel. Every calculation is identical; only some highlight colours shift. Excel or LibreOffice Calc render them as designed.

I bought a workbook and cannot find the download

The download link is in the receipt email from the payment platform, and the platform's account page keeps a copy. Check spam. If it is genuinely gone, email us with the address you paid with and we will send the file directly.

The contact form does not seem to send

The form posts to a third-party form service. If you get no confirmation, email aleksnvkvc@gmail.com directly — it is the same inbox and it is one fewer thing that can go wrong.

Accessibility

Accessibility statement

We aim at WCAG 2.1 level AA and we have not had the site independently audited, so this is a statement of intent and current practice rather than a certification. What is actually in place:

  • Every interactive control is reachable and operable by keyboard, with a visible focus outline
  • Form inputs have real <label> elements, not placeholder text standing in for one
  • Body text is checked for contrast against the dark background; the gold accent is used for emphasis and never as the only way information is conveyed
  • Colour is never the sole signal — a losing load says REJECT as well as turning red
  • Text scales with the browser's font-size setting; nothing is locked to pixels that cannot grow
  • Inputs are at least 16px on mobile so iOS does not zoom on focus
  • Wide tables scroll inside their own container rather than forcing the page sideways

Known gaps: the reveal animations are decorative and currently do not respond to prefers-reduced-motion; the console's denser reports have not been tested with a screen reader. If something here blocks you, email us and it moves to the front of the queue.
