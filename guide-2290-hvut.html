Form 2290, and the stamp you cannot register without

It is one filing a year, a flat $550 for a typical truck, and skipping it stops you from renewing your plates. Here is who owes it, how it is worked out, and the deadline that catches people who bought a truck mid-year.

The Heavy Vehicle Use Tax is a federal road-use tax on heavy trucks, filed once a year on IRS Form 2290. It is small money and simple arithmetic, but it is load-bearing: without the stamped receipt it produces, your state will not renew your registration.

What the Heavy Vehicle Use Tax is

HVUT pays for the wear heavy vehicles put on the interstate system. It is federal, it is annual, and it applies to the truck, not to you — so it follows the vehicle, and a mid-year buyer inherits the obligation for the rest of the tax period.

It is a flat annual tax, not a percentage of anything you earn. A profitable year and a terrible year owe exactly the same $550.

Who has to file

You file Form 2290 if you are the registered owner of a highway vehicle with a taxable gross weight of 55,000 pounds or more. For a standard tractor-trailer that is everyone — a loaded combination runs to 80,000 lb.

  • Owner-operators with their own truck and their own authority
  • Fleets, on every qualifying unit
  • A driver leased to a carrier where the lease makes the driver the registrant — read the lease; the party who registers the plate is usually the party who files

How the tax is worked out

The tax rises with weight, then stops:

Taxable gross weightAnnual tax
Below 55,000 lbno HVUT
55,000 lb (the entry point)$100
Each 1,000 lb over 55,000add $22
75,000 lb and above$550 (the cap)

So a typical 80,000-lb rig pays the $550 maximum. The formula only matters if your truck sits between 55,000 and 75,000 — then it is $100 plus $22 for every thousand pounds above the entry point.

Taxable gross weight is not your scale weight today

It is the fully-equipped weight of the truck plus the trailer it customarily pulls plus the maximum load customarily carried. Not the empty weight, and not whatever the scale reads on one light run. Pick the category honestly — understating it to save a few dollars is the kind of thing that surfaces in an audit.

The deadline, and the mid-year trap

The tax period runs 1 July to 30 June. For a truck you already run, the return is due by 31 August for the year that began that July.

Buy a truck in the middle of the year and your deadline is not August — it is the last day of the month after the month you first put it on the road.

Put it on the road in November, and Form 2290 is due by 31 December, with the tax prorated for the months remaining in the period. This is the single most-missed detail, because people assume the deadline is always August.

Schedule 1: the receipt that unlocks your plates

When the IRS accepts your 2290, it returns a stamped Schedule 1. That document is the proof of payment, and it matters far beyond the tax itself:

  • Your state will not register or renew the plate without it
  • It can be asked for at a roadside inspection
  • A broker or shipper setting you up may want to see it

Keep the current stamped Schedule 1 in the truck and a copy in your files. E-filing returns it in minutes; paper filing takes weeks, which is why almost nobody paper-files any more.

How to file

  1. Get an EIN. You cannot file 2290 with a Social Security number, and a brand new EIN takes about two weeks to become active in the IRS system — do this early.
  2. E-file through an IRS-authorised provider. Filing more than 25 vehicles it is mandatory; below that it is simply faster.
  3. Pay by EFTPS, direct debit, card, or check per the form instructions.
  4. Save the stamped Schedule 1 the moment it comes back.

Low-mileage and suspended vehicles

A vehicle expected to run 5,000 miles or fewer in the period (7,500 for agricultural vehicles) is a suspended vehicle: you still file 2290, but you owe no tax. If it later crosses the mileage limit, the tax becomes due and you file an amendment.

This is genuinely useful for a spare truck or a seasonal unit — but the mileage limit is per the whole period across all owners, so a truck you bought mid-year inherits the miles already run on it.

Four mistakes that cost money

Filing with an SSN instead of an EINrejected
Applying for the EIN the week it is duetwo-week activation delay
Assuming the deadline is always Augustlate penalty on a mid-year truck
Losing the stamped Schedule 1cannot renew the plate

None of these is expensive to avoid and all of them are expensive to fix. HVUT is the definition of a filing you do once, correctly, and forget — put the renewal in the calendar for July.

It is a fixed cost — treat it like one

$550 a year is about half a cent a mile at 110,000 miles. Small, but it belongs in your overhead alongside the plate, the UCR and the permits.
