The rate con shows the rate. It doesn't show what's left.

A broker offers $2.40 a mile. Sounds fine.

Then you add the 250 empty miles to the pickup. The diesel. Six hours at the dock. The 3% factoring fee. The truck payment that's due whether you roll or not.

Once all of that is in, a load that looked good can leave you very little. Sometimes it leaves you nothing.

The usual ways of checking don't catch it:

  • Load board averages are built on other people's costs, not yours.
  • Gut feel works until diesel goes up 40 cents.
  • The spreadsheet you set up last year hasn't been touched since.

What you need is one number: what this load leaves you, with your costs, before you say yes.

Who's doing the math

I'm Aleksandar Novaković. I've worked in U.S. trucking support: load support, track and trace, and day-to-day contact with drivers and brokers. I live in Serbia and work with U.S. carriers by email.

I do every report myself. If something in it looks wrong to you, reply and I'll fix it.

You're in Serbia. Why should I trust you with this?

You don't have to take my word for it. The calculators and guides on this site are free and use the same math I use in the reports. Run a load you already hauled through the calculator. If the result matches what you know about that load, send me the next one.

What if I don't know my costs?

Most people don't, at least not exactly. Send what you have. I'll fill the gaps with reasonable estimates and list every one of them on the report, so you can see what I assumed.

What if the answer is "pass"?

Then the report did its job and kept you off a bad load. The money-back promise is about whether the report helped you decide, not about whether you liked the answer.

How do I pay?

There's no checkout on this site. Once we agree what you need, I send an invoice by email (PayPal or Payoneer), and the report follows once it's paid. See the refund policy.
