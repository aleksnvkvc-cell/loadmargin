A freight decision is an arithmetic problem that nobody has time to do

LoadMargin exists because the gap between a rate that looks acceptable and a rate that actually pays is usually twenty cents a mile — and twenty cents a mile, across a fleet, is the difference between a good year and a lender conversation.

Why it exists

Ask ten carriers what a mile costs them and you will get ten confident answers, most of them wrong by fifteen to twenty-five cents. Not through carelessness — the number is genuinely awkward to compute. It moves when insurance renews, when a truck comes off warranty, when a driver's pay package changes, and when monthly miles drop because a customer went quiet.

So the industry substitutes a proxy: rate per mile. It is easy to say out loud and it is wrong often enough to be dangerous, because it ignores the two costs that decide most loads — deadhead and the fixed cost of simply owning the truck.

A load does not become profitable because the rate per mile sounded good. It becomes profitable when the rate exceeds every cost that the load causes.

LoadMargin is that subtraction, made fast enough to do while a broker is still on the phone.

Who it is for

The tool is the same at every size. What changes is the question. An owner-operator asks should I take this load. A fleet of forty asks which of my dispatchers is quietly booking break-even freight. A carrier of four hundred asks which division, lane and customer combination is destroying margin at scale — and needs the answer in a format a CFO will accept.

Methodology

There is one model and it is public. Every module reads from it; none of them applies a private adjustment.

total_miles = loaded + deadhead
fuel = (total_miles / mpg) × diesel_price
driver = total_miles × pay_cpm | rate × pay_pct
maintenance = total_miles × maintenance_cpm
fixed = total_miles × (monthly_overhead / monthly_miles)
net = rate − (fuel + driver + maintenance + fixed + fees)
margin = net / rate

Three decisions that make it accurate

  • Fuel is modelled in gallons, not dollars per mile. A price spike and an efficiency drop have different causes and different fixes; collapsing them into one number hides both.
  • Deadhead is a first-class cost. Empty miles burn fuel, consume hours of service and carry the same fixed cost per mile as loaded ones. Most models treat them as a footnote.
  • Fixed cost is yours, not the industry's. Overhead divided by your monthly miles. Two carriers on the same lane with the same truck can have a nine-cent gap, and that gap decides the load.

The thresholds — accept at 15% margin, reject below 5% — are defaults, not doctrine. They are the point where a load stops paying for the truck-day it consumes. A carrier with different economics sets their own.

What we deliberately do not claim

Freight software has an honesty problem, so here is the short version of ours.

  • We do not publish a customer count, a dollar figure of "revenue tracked", or an average profit improvement percentage. We would have to invent them, and anyone senior enough to matter can tell.
  • We do not claim live load-board or TMS integration. Bulk import from a TMS export exists on Fleet Licence and above; an API exists on Fleet Scale and above. Live integration is on the roadmap.
  • We do not claim the model predicts the market. It prices the load in front of you against the costs you actually carry. Rate forecasting is a different product and a harder one.
  • We do not use testimonials we cannot attribute to a real, contactable customer.
The calculator is free and unlimited on the homepage precisely because the fastest way to settle an accuracy argument is to let you run last week's load through it.

Data & security

The public calculator sends nothing anywhere. It is client-side JavaScript. Open your browser's network tab while you use it and you will see no outbound request — this is verifiable in thirty seconds, and verifying it beats believing it.

The workbooks send nothing either. They are Excel files. Once downloaded they sit on your computer, they do not phone home, and they keep working whether or not this website is still online.

  • There is no server and no database. This site is static files. There is nowhere for your freight data to be stored even in principle.
  • No accounts, no sign-in, no analytics, no cookies. Nothing to profile you with.
  • No resale, no aggregation, no benchmarking product built from anyone's numbers — there are no numbers here to build one from.
