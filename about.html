Why it exists

Cost per mile is the number every load decision depends on, and it's awkward to work out. It changes when insurance renews, when a truck comes off warranty, when a driver's pay changes, and when monthly miles drop because a customer went quiet.

So most people go by rate per mile instead. It's easy to compare, but it leaves out the two costs that decide a lot of loads: deadhead and the fixed cost of owning the truck.

A load makes money when the rate covers every cost the load causes. A good-sounding rate per mile doesn't guarantee that.

LoadMargin does that subtraction quickly enough to use while a broker is waiting for an answer.

Who it's for

Owner-operators and small fleets that book their own loads and want to know what a load leaves them before they say yes. If you run one truck, the question is usually "should I take this load?" If you run ten, it's also "which lanes and customers actually pay?" Both come down to the same math.

How the math works

There is one model, and it's public. The calculators and the reports I send clients all use it.

total_miles = loaded + deadhead
fuel = (total_miles / mpg) × diesel_price
driver = total_miles × pay_cpm | rate × pay_pct
maintenance = total_miles × maintenance_cpm
fixed = total_miles × (monthly_overhead / monthly_miles)
net = rate − (fuel + driver + maintenance + fixed + fees)
margin = net / rate

Three choices behind it

  • Fuel is worked out in gallons. A higher diesel price and a drop in MPG have different causes and different fixes, so the model keeps them apart.
  • Deadhead counts as fully as loaded miles. Empty miles burn the same fuel, use the same hours and carry the same fixed cost per mile.
  • Fixed cost is yours. Your monthly overhead divided by your monthly miles, not an industry average.

The calculator's default thresholds (accept at 15% margin, reject below 5%) are only starting points. You can set your own, and a report uses the target margin you give me.

What I don't claim

  • LoadMargin is new. There are no client numbers, savings figures or reviews on this site, because I'd have to make them up. When there are real ones, they'll be shown with the client's permission.
  • There is no load-board or TMS integration. You type the numbers in, or email them to me.
  • The model doesn't predict rates. It prices the load in front of you against the costs you actually carry.
  • A report is an analysis of the numbers you give me, not a guarantee of profit and not tax or legal advice.
The quickest way to judge the math is to run a load you already hauled through the free calculator and see if the answer matches what you know.

Your data

The calculators send nothing anywhere. They run in your browser. You can check this by opening your browser's network tab while you use one: there are no outgoing requests.

For a paid service, the numbers you email me are used only to do that work. They aren't shared, sold or pooled with anyone else's. The privacy policy says what is kept and for how long.

The site itself has no accounts, no analytics and no tracking cookies.
