Fuel cards, and the discount that is not always a discount

A fuel card can save real money or quietly cost you, depending on how the discount is structured and where you actually fuel. Here is how to read a fuel-card offer past the headline 'cents off' and pick the one that pays at your stops.

Fuel is your biggest variable cost, so a card that shaves cents a gallon sounds like free money. Sometimes it is. But the headline discount hides how it is calculated, where it applies and what fees ride along — and a card that looks generous can pay less than a plain one at the stops you actually use.

What a fuel card is

A fuel card is a payment card, usually tied to a network of truck stops, that gives negotiated pricing, tracks your fuel purchases, and often bundles reporting (handy for IFTA) and controls. Some are tied to a factoring company or a carrier program; some are standalone.

Retail-minus vs cost-plus

The single most important thing to understand — the same distinction that decides whether the deal is good:

TypeWhat it meansBehaviour
Retail minusA discount off the stop's posted cash priceLooks huge at expensive stops; a high posted price can swallow the discount
Cost plusThe stop's cost of the fuel plus a small fixed marginNarrower headline, far more predictable, usually better where you have volume

“50 cents off” retail-minus at a stop posting 40 cents above the local average is a ten-cent saving. Judge a card on the final price paid, not the advertised discount.

The discount is only real on the network

A card's pricing applies at its network stops. A brilliant rate at chains that are not on your lanes is not a rate you will ever get. Before choosing, check the network against where you actually fuel — and remember fuel is best bought net of tax, which the card's network may or may not line up with.

The fees that eat the saving

Read the fee schedule before the discount

  • Transaction fees per fill — a fixed fee is a real per-gallon cost on small buys
  • Monthly or account fees
  • ATM / cash advance fees if you pull cash
  • Whether the discount is capped by gallons per transaction or per month

A card with a great discount and a $2 transaction fee, used twice a day, gives back a chunk of the saving in fees. Net it out before you believe the headline.

Fuel cards, credit and cash flow

Some fuel cards are credit (billed weekly) and some are prepaid/debit. A credit fuel card can smooth cash flow — you fuel now, pay on terms — which matters when you are waiting 30 days for a broker to pay. It can also help build business credit. A debit card avoids debt but ties up cash. Match it to your cash-flow reality.

How to compare cards honestly

  1. List the stops you actually use on your lanes.
  2. For each card, work out the real price per gallon at those stops — retail-minus vs cost-plus, after fees.
  3. Add the transaction and monthly fees at your fill frequency.
  4. Check credit vs prepaid against your cash flow.
  5. Value the reporting — a card that produces your IFTA mileage and gallons saves real hours.

The best card is not the one with the biggest number on the flyer — it is the one that pays least per gallon at your stops after fees. Do the boring comparison once and it pays every fill.

Fuel is the number worth chasing

Card savings, buying net of tax and half an mpg all stack up on your single biggest variable cost. Put your real fuel price into the calculator, not the pump average.
