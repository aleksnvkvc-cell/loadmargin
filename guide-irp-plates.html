IRP, the plate that bills you by where you drive

An apportioned plate is not a normal registration — it divides your fee across every state by the miles you run there. Get the mileage records right and it is simple; get them wrong and the renewal or an audit surprises you.

The International Registration Plan is how one plate covers interstate operation across many states, with the registration fee split among them by the miles you run in each. It is not complicated once you see the logic, but it runs on mileage records — and carriers who do not keep them well pay too much or get an ugly audit.

What IRP is

Instead of registering your truck separately in every state you drive through, IRP gives you one apportioned plate and cab card from your base state that is valid across all member jurisdictions. The single fee is divided among the states according to how much you drive in each.

One plate, many states, and a fee split by your miles. IRP registers the truck; it is not a fuel tax and not operating authority — those are separate.

How apportionment works

You report the miles you expect to run (or did run) in each member state. Your fee is the sum of each state's registration cost weighted by your percentage of miles there. Run mostly in three states and most of your fee goes to those three. It is a fair split — you pay each state roughly for the use you make of its roads.

Your first year is an estimate

A new carrier has no mileage history, so the first year uses estimated distances the plan provides for your base state. At renewal, you switch to your actual miles from the reporting period. This is why year-two IRP can look different from year one — you have moved from an estimate to your real driving.

The mileage records that decide it

Your renewal and your audit both run on the same mileage records

You must keep detailed distance records by jurisdiction — the same records that feed IFTA. Trip sheets or, far better, GPS/ELD mileage by state. Sloppy records mean a wrong apportionment and a bad audit. Good records make both filings almost automatic.

IRP and IFTA are cousins, not the same

IRP apportions your registration fee; IFTA settles your fuel tax. Both split something across states by your miles, both are filed through your base state, and both run on the same mileage records — which is why people confuse them. They are separate obligations with separate filings. Keep one clean set of by-state mileage and it serves both.

The audit, and why records matter

States audit IRP (and IFTA) accounts, and the audit is a records exercise: can you prove the miles you claimed in each state? Reconstructed or estimated mileage that does not match fuel and ELD data is where assessments come from. The defence is the same as everywhere on this site — contemporaneous records, kept as you go.

A short checklist

Apportioned plate and cab card from your base state✓
By-jurisdiction mileage kept every trip (GPS/ELD ideal)✓
Renewal uses actual miles, not estimates✓
Records reconcile with IFTA and fuel✓

Registration is fixed overhead

IRP, plate and permits run whether the truck moves or not. Put the annual total in your cost per mile.
