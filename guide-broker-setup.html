Getting set up with brokers, the first time and fast

Before a broker gives you freight they set you up in their system. Doing it cleanly and quickly is the difference between booking the load and watching it go to the next carrier. Here is exactly what they need and how to be ready.

A broker will not release a load to a carrier who is not set up in their system. Setup is a paperwork step, and how fast and cleanly you clear it decides whether you get the load or the broker moves on to someone who is ready. It is worth being ready.

Why setup speed wins loads

When a broker has a load and calls you, they usually have other carriers to call too. If your packet comes back complete in minutes, you are booked. If it takes a day of back-and-forth for a missing document, the load is gone. Being the carrier who is ready is a real, unglamorous competitive edge.

The load does not go to the best carrier. It goes to the carrier who was set up and reachable when the broker needed a truck.

What is in a carrier packet

The broker sends a “carrier packet” — a set of forms to complete once per broker:

  • Carrier information — legal name, DOT/MC, address, contact
  • W-9 — so they can pay you and report it
  • A copy of your operating authority
  • A certificate of insurance (see below — usually your insurer sends this)
  • A signed broker-carrier agreement — the master terms that sit behind every rate con with that broker; read it, especially the payment and liability clauses
  • Banking or factoring details for payment

The filings a broker checks behind the scenes

Before or as they set you up, a broker verifies:

  • Your authority is active on FMCSA’s system — not revoked, not inactive
  • Your insurance is on file and adequate — typically $1,000,000 auto liability and $100,000 cargo, filed by your insurer
  • Your safety record — a very poor CSA can make some brokers decline you
  • That you are not a double-brokering or fraud risk — increasingly checked after recent fraud waves

Have this ready before you call

Keep a folder, digital and ready to send in one message:

Operating authority letterPDF
Certificate of insurance (or your agent on standby to send it)PDF
Completed W-9PDF

A signed copy of your standard details for the packet formsready

With this ready, most setups clear in minutes.

The factoring notice of assignment

If you factor your invoices, the broker must be told to pay the factor, not you, via a notice of assignment (NOA). Your factor provides it. Setting this up wrong — the broker pays you when they were supposed to pay the factor, or vice versa — causes real payment tangles, so get the NOA into the setup from the start.

Being the easy carrier to set up

  1. Keep every document current and in one folder.
  2. Answer setup emails the same hour, not the same week.
  3. Read the broker-carrier agreement once per broker — the terms carry across all their loads.
  4. Keep your FMCSA record clean and your insurance filed, so the behind-the-scenes checks pass without a hitch.

Setup is fixed effort, not per-load

You set up once per broker, then book freely. Front-load the paperwork so the next load is a phone call, not a day of forms.
