Banking and books, the habit that pays for itself

Every number on this site depends on knowing what you spent. That comes from one unglamorous habit: a separate business account and books you keep as you go, not in a panic in April. Here is the minimum that actually works.

Bookkeeping is where most owner-operators lose money without noticing — not to theft, to not knowing. If you cannot say what you spent, you cannot know your cost per mile, you cannot price a load, and you overpay tax because you cannot prove your deductions. The fix is a habit, not a talent.

Why this is not optional

Three separate things all depend on clean books:

  • Your cost per mile — the number every load decision rests on — is only as good as your expense records
  • Your LLC protection only holds if business and personal money stay separate
  • Your tax bill is only as low as the deductions you can prove
You cannot manage a number you do not measure. The carrier who does not keep books is guessing at their own business — usually in the wrong direction.

The separate account comes first

Open a business checking account and run every business dollar through it — every load payment in, every fuel and repair and permit out. Get a business debit or credit card for expenses. Never pay for groceries from it, and never pay for diesel from your personal account.

This one step does three jobs at once: it keeps the LLC wall real, it makes bookkeeping almost automatic (the statement is most of your books), and it makes an audit survivable.

The categories that matter for a truck

Track expenses in categories that map to how a trucking business actually works — because these are your cost-per-mile inputs and your deductions:

Fuelthe biggest variable cost
Maintenance & repairs, tyresthe reserve that ambushes people
Truck payment / leasefixed
Insurancefixed
Permits, plates, UCR, HVUT, IFTAfixed compliance
Tolls, scales, parkingroad costs
Meals / per diem (track the days)a big deduction
Phone, subscriptions, ELD, factoring feesoverhead

Receipts, the part everyone hates

The IRS wants proof. The good news: a business card statement covers most of it, and per diem needs proof of the days away (your ELD has that), not a receipt for every meal. For the rest:

  • Photograph a paper receipt the moment you get it — a shoebox of faded thermal paper is not records
  • Keep them attached to the month, digitally, so you can find one in an audit
  • Cash spending without a receipt is a deduction you cannot defend — put it on the card

How to actually keep the books

You do not need enterprise software. Options, cheapest first:

  1. A disciplined spreadsheet — income and expenses by category and month. Enough for one truck if you actually update it weekly.
  2. Trucking-specific bookkeeping software — imports the bank feed and categorises, often with IFTA mileage built in.
  3. A bookkeeper — hand it off once volume makes your time worth more than the fee. This is a service a carrier will happily pay for.

The rule that makes any of them work: weekly, not yearly

Fifteen minutes every week beats two agonising weeks in April, and it means you always know your numbers — so a load decision is informed today, not reconstructed next spring. The tool barely matters; the weekly rhythm is the whole game.

Reconciling, and reading your own numbers

Once a month, match your records to the bank statement so nothing is missed or double-counted. Then actually read them: revenue, cost per mile, profit per truck-day. Books you keep and never look at are half the value. The point is not the tax return — it is knowing, every month, whether the business is working.

Why clean books cut your tax bill

Every legitimate business expense you can prove reduces your taxable profit. Miss them — the small tolls, the phone, the parking, the per-diem days — and you pay tax on money you actually spent on the business. Clean books do not just keep you legal; they are worth real money at tax time, often more than the bookkeeping costs.

Your books ARE your cost per mile

Feed real, categorised numbers into the calculator and the verdict on every load is real too. Guessed inputs give a confident, wrong answer.
