How the cost per mile calculator works

Formula & FAQ +

Cost per mile is what every mile costs you before it earns anything. This trucking cost per mile calculator adds two parts. Fixed costs (truck and trailer payments, insurance, permits, software) are the same every month, so they are divided by the miles you actually run, loaded and empty. Variable costs (fuel, driver pay, maintenance) grow with every mile.

The total is your break-even rate per mile. Any load that pays less, counting its empty miles, loses money. The rate for your target margin is break-even divided by one minus the margin. The calculator starts at $6.28 diesel, the U.S. average on 19 October 2026 (EIA); change it to what you pay.

fixed cost per mile = monthly fixed costs ÷ monthly miles
fuel per mile = diesel price ÷ MPG
cost per mile = fixed + fuel + driver pay + maintenance per mile
rate for target margin = cost per mile ÷ (1 − margin)

What is a typical trucking cost per mile?

It varies a lot with diesel, truck payment and how many miles you run. At October 2026 diesel prices, many owner-operators land above $2.00 a mile once fuel and fixed costs are counted. Your own figure is the only one that matters for your loads.

Why divide by all miles, not just loaded miles?

Because the truck burns fuel and wears out on empty miles too. Dividing by loaded miles only makes your cost per mile look lower than it is.
