Reference

Freight glossary

36 terms used across this site and across the industry, defined without jargon. Where a definition has arithmetic behind it, the arithmetic is here too.

A B C D E F H L M N O R S T V

A

Accessorial

Any charge on top of the line haul: detention, lumper, tarping, layover, a second stop. They are real revenue and belong in the load's revenue figure — but a charge that is owed and never paid is not revenue, it is a receivable.

All-in rate per mile

Revenue divided by total miles, loaded plus deadhead. The only version of rate per mile worth comparing between loads. See why the usual version misleads.

B

Backhaul

A load taken on the return leg of a trip. Should always be evaluated as part of the whole round trip, never on its own — the alternative to a cheap backhaul is not zero, it is the cost of driving home empty.

Break-even rate

The rate at which a load exactly covers its own costs and earns nothing. Below it, the load costs you money to run. Typically around $1.92 a mile all-in for a single-truck operation.

Broker

An intermediary who arranges freight between a shipper and a carrier, taking a margin. Not the enemy — but their margin and yours come out of the same rate.

C

Cost per mile (CPM)

What one mile costs your truck, all in. Fixed cost per mile plus fuel, maintenance and driver pay. Full guide here.

CSA score

The FMCSA's Safety Measurement System rating. Visible to brokers and insurers, and it moves your insurance premium — which is why an hours-of-service violation is a cost-per-mile problem, not just a compliance one.

Cycle (60/70)

The rolling limit on total on-duty hours: 60 in any 7 consecutive days, or 70 in any 8. Resets only with a 34-hour restart. More here.

D

Deadhead

Miles run empty. Costs exactly what a loaded mile costs, minus a small fuel saving from the lighter trailer. Roughly $1.90 a mile at a typical cost base. Full guide here.

Detention

Time a truck spends waiting at a shipper or receiver beyond the free period. Costs money whether or not it is billed — the truck is consuming fixed cost by the hour and burning the 14-hour window.

Dispatch

The function that assigns loads to trucks. Where most margin is won or lost, because it is where the accept-or-decline decision is actually made.

Dry van

A standard enclosed trailer. The most common equipment type and usually the most competitive freight.

E

ELD

Electronic Logging Device. Records duty status automatically and is the legal record of hours of service. A planning tool is not an ELD and cannot replace one.

F

Fixed cost

Costs charged whether the truck moves or not: truck and trailer payments, insurance, permits, ELD subscription, parking, accounting. Divided by monthly miles to get fixed cost per mile — the figure most carriers underestimate.

Flatbed

Open-deck equipment for freight that cannot go in a van. Higher rates, more work, more weather exposure. ATRI recorded the flatbed sector at a −0.5% operating margin in 2025.

FMCSA

Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. The US regulator that sets hours-of-service rules and maintains the CSA scoring system.

H

Hours of service (HOS)

The federal limits on driving and duty time. Four clocks run at once: 11 hours driving, a 14-hour window, a 30-minute break after 8 hours of driving, and the 60/70-hour cycle. Full guide here.

L

Lane

An origin–destination pair, run repeatedly. The right unit for measuring profitability, because patterns show up across a lane that no single load reveals. Rank lanes by profit per truck-day, not by revenue.

Line haul

The base rate for moving the freight, before accessorials. What the broker quotes first.

Loaded miles

Miles run with freight on. What the rate confirmation shows, and what almost everyone divides by when they quote rate per mile — which is the problem.

Lumper

A third party paid to load or unload a trailer, usually at a grocery or food distribution centre. Paid out of pocket by the driver and reimbursed, or not.

M

Merchant of record

The party legally selling to the buyer. When a platform acts as merchant of record it collects payment, calculates and remits VAT or sales tax, and issues the receipt — the software vendor is its supplier rather than your seller.

MPG

Miles per gallon. Model fuel as gallons rather than cents per mile: it separates a price rise from an efficiency drop, which have completely different fixes.

N

Net profit

What is left after every cost the load causes: fuel, driver pay, maintenance, fixed cost, fees and the cost of time spent sitting. Not revenue minus fuel.

O

Operating margin

Net profit as a percentage of revenue. ATRI put US truckload and refrigerated sectors below 1.0% for 2025 — which is why a twenty-cent error in cost per mile decides whether a year works.

Out-of-route miles

Miles driven above the planned route. Wrong turns, avoided scales, a detour for a preferred fuel stop. Costs the same as any other mile and is one of the few things a driver directly controls.

Owner-operator

A driver who owns their truck and runs it as a business, either under their own authority or leased to a carrier.

R

Rate confirmation

The document confirming the agreed rate and terms for a load. Shows loaded miles. Does not show your deadhead, which is exactly why deadhead gets forgotten.

Rate per mile (RPM)

Rate divided by miles. Useful only if you are honest about which miles. Divided by loaded miles it flatters every load; divided by total miles it tells the truth. See all-in rate per mile.

Reefer

Refrigerated trailer. Higher rates, higher costs — the reefer unit burns its own fuel and needs its own maintenance.

S

Sleeper berth split

Dividing the required 10 hours off into two periods (7/3 or 8/2), neither of which counts against the 14-hour window. Real flexibility around an awkward appointment.

Spot market

Freight priced load by load rather than under contract. Higher variance in both directions, and where a load-scoring tool earns its keep.

T

34-hour restart

34 consecutive hours off duty, which resets the 60/70-hour cycle to zero. No limit on how often it can be used.

TMS

Transportation Management System. The software a carrier runs its operation on — dispatch, billing, documents. LoadMargin does not replace one and does not currently integrate with one.

Truck-day

One truck, one day of capacity. The scarce resource a carrier actually sells, and the right denominator for ranking lanes. A three-day lane earning $900 loses to a one-day lane earning $500.

V

Variable cost

Costs incurred per mile driven: fuel, tyres, maintenance, per-mile driver pay, tolls. Runs whether the trailer is loaded or empty.
