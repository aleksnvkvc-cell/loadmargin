The drug and alcohol program, the part behind the database

The Clearinghouse is the database; the program is the machine that feeds it. Every carrier must run one, and an owner-operator cannot run their own. Here is what a compliant program actually contains, beyond registering online.

The Clearinghouse is the federal database of violations. Behind it every carrier must operate an actual drug and alcohol testing program — the consortium, the tests, the policy, the training and the records. Registering online is not the program; it is one part of it. Here is the whole machine.

Why a program, not just a login

DOT rules require carriers with CDL drivers to run a testing program to defined standards. It is what the Clearinghouse records the results of. Skipping the program is one of the automatic failures at a new entrant audit — nearly a third of the sixteen live here. So this is foundational, not optional.

The consortium, and why you cannot self-administer

An owner-operator must join a C/TPA

A one-driver operation cannot run its own random-testing pool — you cannot randomly select yourself credibly. You must enrol in a consortium / third-party administrator (C/TPA) that runs the random pool, arranges collections and manages the process, and you must designate it in the Clearinghouse. Budget roughly $50–$150 a year for membership plus the cost of tests.

The six test types

TestTrigger
Pre-employmentBefore any safety-sensitive work (drug test)
RandomUnannounced, through the consortium, spread across the year
Post-accidentAfter a qualifying accident (see the accident guide)
Reasonable suspicionOn a trained supervisor's specific, contemporaneous observation
Return-to-dutyAfter a violation, once the SAP process is complete (observed)
Follow-upUnannounced, on the SAP's schedule after return (observed)

The drug panel covers marijuana, cocaine, amphetamines, opioids and PCP — and marijuana stays prohibited under federal rules regardless of state law.

The written policy

You must have a written drug and alcohol policy that meets DOT requirements and is provided to drivers — covering prohibited conduct, the testing types, the consequences, and the return-to-duty process. “We test” is not a policy; the document is a requirement, and a missing one is a finding.

Supervisor reasonable-suspicion training

If you employ drivers, supervisors who could make a reasonable-suspicion determination must complete the required training — typically an hour on drugs and an hour on alcohol — so a suspicion referral holds up. A solo owner-operator does not supervise anyone, but the moment you hire, this applies.

Records and retention

  • Consortium enrolment and C/TPA designation in the Clearinghouse
  • Test results and the random selection records
  • The written policy and driver acknowledgements
  • Supervisor training certificates, where applicable
  • The annual Clearinghouse query, kept three years

These are exactly what an auditor asks for. Keep them together, as a live file, not something to assemble in a panic.

The program checklist

Enrolled in a C/TPA and designated in the Clearinghouse✓
Pre-employment test done before driving✓
In a random pool✓
Written policy in place and given to drivers✓
Supervisor training (if you employ drivers)✓
Annual Clearinghouse query, kept 3 years✓

Compliance is fixed overhead

Consortium fees and testing run whether the truck moves or not. Put them in your cost per mile with the permits and plates.
