Buying a truck without buying a problem

The truck is the biggest decision you make and the easiest to get wrong. New or used, financing that does not strangle you, and the inspection that separates a good used truck from a $20,000 mistake — here is how to buy the one that earns.

The truck is your largest purchase and your production line. Buy the right one and it quietly earns for years; buy the wrong one and it eats every load in repairs and downtime. The mistake is almost always the same — buying on the sticker price instead of the cost to own.

The frame: the truck is a tool, not a trophy

A shiny truck that keeps you broke is a bad truck. The only questions that matter are: will it run reliably, what will it truly cost to own per mile, and does the payment survive a slow month. Chrome is not on the list.

Two carriers buy the same-priced truck. One buys a clean, boring, well-maintained unit; the other buys a pretty one with a tired engine. A year later they are in completely different businesses.

Most new carriers buy used, because the capital and the payment are survivable. That is fine — if you buy a good used truck. The whole risk of used is that you inherit someone else's maintenance, good or bad. So the inspection is everything.

Financing reality for a new authority

A brand-new authority with no business credit is the hardest borrower to finance. Expect:

  • A larger down payment and a higher interest rate than an established carrier — the lender is pricing your lack of record
  • Lenders who specialise in owner-operator and semi-truck financing, not a regular bank
  • The temptation of a lease-purchase instead — read that guide before you sign one; the gross is not the story

The payment has to survive a bad month

The number that matters is not whether you can make the payment in a good week — it is whether you can make it in a slow one, with a repair bill in the same month. A payment that only works when everything goes right is a payment that will eventually not work. Buy the truck whose payment you can carry through a soft market, not the one you can just afford on a great week.

The pre-purchase inspection

Never buy a used truck without a pre-purchase inspection by a diesel mechanic you chose — not the seller's. It costs a few hundred dollars and it is the best money in the whole purchase. It should cover:

  • Engine — oil analysis, blow-by, leaks, and the ECM data for miles and fault history
  • Aftertreatment — DPF/DEF system condition; this is a huge repair cost if it is failing (see the emissions guide)
  • Transmission and clutch, brakes, tyres, frame and rust
  • Service records — a truck with documented maintenance is worth a premium over one with a story

Engines and the miles question

A Class 8 diesel is built for high mileage — 700,000 to over a million miles with care — so a used truck at 500,000 miles is not automatically old. What matters is how those miles were run and maintained, and whether a major service (like an overhaul) is looming. A well-kept high-mile truck can beat a neglected lower-mile one. Judge the maintenance, not just the odometer.

The number that matters: cost to own

Add it all up per mile, not per sticker:

  • The payment spread over your monthly miles
  • Expected maintenance and repairs for a truck of that age and mileage — see the maintenance guide
  • Insurance for that unit
  • Its fuel economy, which varies more than people think

A cheaper truck with worse fuel economy and a tired engine can cost more per mile than a dearer, cleaner one. The sticker is the down payment on the truth, not the truth.
