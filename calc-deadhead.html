How the deadhead calculator works

Formula & FAQ +

Deadhead is the empty miles a truck drives with no paying freight: to the pickup, or home after the delivery. Those miles burn the same fuel and carry the same truck payment as loaded ones, so this deadhead calculator charges every mile at your all-in cost per mile.

For a return load it works out the cost of the whole leg, empty miles to the pickup plus loaded miles home, and subtracts it from the rate. It then compares that with the cost of simply driving home empty. A backhaul that loses money on its own can still be the better choice if it loses less than running empty.

cost of running empty = miles home × cost per mile
return leg net = rate − (loaded + deadhead miles) × cost per mile
better than empty = return leg net + cost of running empty

What is a good deadhead percentage?

Many owner-operators aim to keep empty miles under 10–15% of all miles. Above that, check every load's rate per total mile, not just per loaded mile.

Should I count deadhead in my rate per mile?

Yes. Divide the rate by loaded plus empty miles. That is the rate the truck actually earns.
