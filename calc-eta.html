How the HOS and ETA calculator works

Formula & FAQ +

This hours-of-service calculator checks whether a load can be delivered legally and on time. It divides the remaining miles by a realistic average speed to get driving time, then adds a 30-minute break if the drive pushes you past 8 hours of driving since your last break.

It checks the result against the 11-hour driving limit and the 14-hour on-duty window, and tells you the farthest you can legally drive right now. It is a planning tool for property-carrying drivers, not a replacement for your ELD.

driving time = miles ÷ average speed
30-minute break needed when driving since last break + driving time > 8 hours
legal = driving time ≤ driving hours left, and total time ≤ on-duty hours left

Does it include the 70-hour rule?

No. It checks the 11-hour, 14-hour and 30-minute rules. Check your 60/70-hour cycle on your ELD.

What speed should I enter?

Your real average over the trip, including slow sections, usually well below the speed limit. 50–55 mph is common for long runs.
