Accessorials, the charges you keep forgetting to bill

Beyond the line-haul rate is a whole list of charges for the extra work you do — and small carriers leave most of it unbilled. Here is the master list, why each goes uncollected, and the habit that turns unpaid work back into revenue.

The line-haul rate pays for driving the freight. Everything else you do — waiting, extra stops, helping unload, a wasted trip — is billable through accessorial charges, and small carriers leave most of it on the table. It is unpaid work you already did; the only reason it goes uncollected is that nobody set it up to be billed.

What accessorials are

Accessorials are charges for services and costs beyond moving the load point to point. Detention is the famous one, but it is only the start. Each represents real time, cost or work — and each is money you are entitled to when it is agreed and documented.

The line-haul is what you agreed to do. Accessorials are what actually happened. The gap between them is revenue most carriers give away for free.

The master list

AccessorialWhat it is for
DetentionWaiting beyond the free time at a shipper or receiver
LayoverBeing held overnight, unable to work the truck
TONUTruck Ordered Not Used — you showed up and the load fell through
Lumper feePaying a third party to load/unload — reimbursed
Driver assist / unloadYou physically helping load or unload
Extra stopsAdditional pickups or drops beyond the first
RedeliveryComing back after a receiver could not take it
Detour / out-of-routeMiles beyond the planned route at the broker's request
Reefer / fuel for temp controlThe cost of running the reefer unit
TarpingThe work and equipment of tarping a flatbed load

Why they go uncollected

Three reasons, all fixable:

  • Not agreed up front — if it is not on the rate con, the broker can say it is not owed
  • Not documented — you cannot bill detention you cannot prove; timestamps are the evidence
  • Not billed — the driver moves on to the next load and never invoices the extra, because it is a hassle and the line-haul already paid

Get them on the rate con first

The time to agree an accessorial is before it happens

Detention rate, layover, extra-stop pay, TONU — the terms should be on the rate confirmation before you accept the load. An accessorial you agreed in writing is one you bill with confidence; one you spring on the broker afterward is one you argue about and often lose. Ask for the terms up front — most brokers will put reasonable ones on — and if they refuse detention entirely, that tells you about the load.

Document, then bill

For anything time-based, the evidence is timestamps: arrival and departure, from your ELD, signed on the paperwork where you can. For costs like lumpers, keep the receipt. For extra stops and assist, note them on the BOL. Then actually put them on the invoice — the single biggest fix is simply billing the accessorial instead of letting it go because the line-haul cleared.

Actually collecting

Invoice the accessorials with the documentation attached, promptly, as part of the load's billing. If a broker disputes a well-documented, pre-agreed charge, that is information about the broker for next time. The carriers who collect accessorials are not more aggressive — they are just more organised: they agreed it up front, documented it as it happened, and billed it without fail.

Accessorials in your number

Uncollected accessorials quietly lower your real rate per mile below what you think you are earning. A load that paid well on the line-haul but cost you three unbilled hours of detention paid less than you booked. Track what you bill and what you collect, and price dwell risk into loads that carry it. The accessorials you collect are often the difference between a load that paid and one that only looked like it did.

Bill the work you actually did

The line-haul is the plan; accessorials are what happened. Agree them up front, document them, and bill every one — it is revenue you already earned.
