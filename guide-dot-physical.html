The DOT physical, and the card you cannot drive without

Your medical certificate is as load-bearing as your CDL: let it lapse and you are out of service, and your licence can be downgraded. Here is what the exam covers, what shortens a card, and how to keep the certificate from ambushing you.

To drive a commercial vehicle you must hold a valid medical certificate — the “medical card” — from a DOT exam. It is not a formality: an expired card puts you out of service and can get your CDL downgraded, and certain health findings can shorten it to a year or less. Treating it as a date to manage, not a box to tick, keeps it from stranding you.

What the DOT physical is

A DOT physical is performed by a medical examiner listed on FMCSA's National Registry. Pass, and you get a medical examiner's certificate valid for up to two years — sometimes less, depending on your health. You cannot legally drive commercially without a current one.

What the exam checks

The examiner is assessing whether you can safely operate a truck. Typically:

  • Vision — a minimum standard, with corrective lenses allowed
  • Hearing — a defined standard
  • Blood pressure — high readings can shorten the card or require follow-up
  • General health — heart, lungs, neurological, and a review of your history and medications
  • A urinalysis for certain indicators (this is not the drug test)

Conditions that shorten or threaten the card

Some conditions mean a one-year card or a monitoring requirement

Well-managed conditions usually do not end a driving career, but they can shorten the certificate or attach requirements. Examples handled routinely: controlled blood pressure (often a one-year card), managed diabetes, and treated sleep apnoea (often requiring proof the treatment is used). The theme is management and documentation — a controlled, documented condition is usually workable; an ignored one is the risk.

The practical point: if you have a condition that affects the card, stay ahead of it — keep it managed, bring the documentation, and know your card may be annual so you plan the renewals more often. Health details are yours and private; the only reason they appear here is that the certificate is a legal requirement to drive.

How the card ties to your CDL

Your medical certificate status is tied to your CDL through your state licensing agency. You (or the process, depending on your state) must ensure your current medical status is on file with the state. If your certificate lapses in the state's records, your CDL can be downgraded — the commercial privilege removed — even if you have a paper card in your wallet.

The lapse that puts you out of service

An expired medical certificate is a driver out-of-service condition at a roadside inspection and a violation on your record. It is also entirely predictable — you know the expiry date the day you get the card. Nobody should ever be surprised by a medical-card lapse, and yet it is a common finding, because people treat a two-year date as far away right up until it is not.

Keeping the card from ambushing you

  1. Diary the expiry the day you get the card, with a reminder 60 days before.
  2. Book the exam early — do not drive on the last valid day.
  3. Confirm the certificate reaches your state so your CDL is not downgraded.
  4. Keep the card in the truck and a copy in your files.
  5. If your card is annual, treat renewals as a yearly event, not a distant one.

The card is a date, so manage it like one

An out-of-service medical lapse costs driving days and a CSA violation. It is the most avoidable violation there is — put the expiry in the calendar the day you pass.
