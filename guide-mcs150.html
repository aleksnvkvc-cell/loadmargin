The MCS-150, the update that keeps you legal

Every carrier must refresh its USDOT registration every two years, even if nothing changed. Miss it and your number is deactivated and you can be fined. Here is the schedule, what it feeds, and why the numbers on it matter beyond the form.

The MCS-150 is the form that keeps your USDOT registration current. It is required every two years whether or not anything changed, on a schedule set by your USDOT number itself. It is easy, free and forgettable — and forgetting it deactivates your number and can cost you a fine.

What the MCS-150 is

The MCS-150 (the Motor Carrier Identification Report) is how FMCSA keeps carrier information current: your operation, your fleet size, your mileage, your contact details. Every carrier with a USDOT number must file it, and must update it at least every two years, even with no changes.

When yours is due

The schedule is hidden in your USDOT number

The update month is set by the next-to-last digit of your USDOT number, and whether you file this year or next by whether the last digit is odd or even. So the deadline is not the same date for everyone — it is derived from your own number. Look it up once, and put the month in your calendar; it does not move.

You also must update it whenever key information changes — a new address, a big change in fleet size — not only on the two-year cycle.

What happens if you miss it

  • Your USDOT number is deactivated — which can cascade into your operating authority and stop you from operating legally
  • You can be fined for failing to update
  • Reactivating is extra hassle at exactly the wrong moment — usually when a broker or inspector has just noticed

For a free two-minute update, that is a bad trade. It sits alongside UCR and the Clearinghouse query as a cheap filing that becomes an expensive problem only when ignored.

What the MCS-150 feeds

The numbers you put on it are not just paperwork — they are used elsewhere:

  • Your power-unit count can drive your UCR bracket — an inflated count means a bigger UCR bill
  • Your mileage feeds safety measurement calculations
  • Your contact details are how FMCSA and brokers reach and verify you

So keep it accurate, not just current. A stale or wrong MCS-150 quietly distorts other things.

Updating it, and when to do it off-schedule

You file it through FMCSA's registration system. Update it off-cycle when you:

  1. Change your business address or contact
  2. Significantly change fleet size or operation type
  3. Need current information to show a broker or for another filing

It is free to update directly with FMCSA — be wary of third-party sites charging a fee to file a form you can file yourself in minutes.

A short checklist

Know your update month (from your USDOT digits)✓
Diary it every two years✓
Power-unit count accurate (it sets UCR)✓
Address and contact current✓
File direct with FMCSA, not a paid middleman✓

Bundle it with the year-end filings

The MCS-150, UCR and the Clearinghouse query are all cheap, all forgettable, and all expensive when missed. Handle them together on a fixed date.
